# Attempt B — Precedent check, then dimensional transmutation on the Lorentzian branch

**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Type:** Position (explorer fan-out, width 3), Attempt B
**Question:** With a no-boundary Euclidean cap whose only dimensionful inputs are $(\Lambda, G)$, can the branch structure generate any scale other than $\Lambda^{1/2}$ (equivalently the repo's Starobinsky $H_0$)? If not, where does the second scale come from?

## Position, in one paragraph

**(Commit.)** No established result produces, from a no-boundary cap with inputs $(\Lambda, G)$ alone, a scale exponentially separated from both $\Lambda^{1/2}$ and $M_P$. Every cap-side or branch-side scale in the verified literature is *polynomial* in the inputs: $H_0 \sim \Lambda^{1/2}$, $H_0 \sim M_P/\sqrt{N}$ (trace anomaly), $H(\phi_0) \sim m\phi_0$ (a modulus, needing $m$ as input), $m_S \sim \sqrt{\xi f^4/8\pi^2}\,M_P$ (gravitational Coleman–Weinberg, a loop factor). The one mechanism in established physics that manufactures $e^{-1/g^2}$ is dimensional transmutation of an asymptotically free coupling, and it already lives inside the framework's own central object: the trace anomaly has a *gauge* term $\frac{\beta(g)}{2g}F^2$ alongside the *curvature* terms $aE_4 + cC^2$. The curvature terms fix $H_0$ on the cap (this is Hawking–Hertog–Reall's no-boundary Starobinsky instanton); the gauge term fixes hadron masses on the Lorentzian branch (Collins–Duncan–Joglekar; Ji), and that *is* transmutation. My position is therefore: **the hierarchy is generated on the branch, not at the root; the root weights it flatly.** The junction supplies the matching scale $\mu_* = H_0$ and the $\beta$-function coefficients (through the same field content that fixes $a, c$), but not the coupling's boundary value $g^2(H_0)$. That one number is the precise second datum the junction must carry, and I say below what size it has to be and where it could come from.

## 1. Precedent (all verified by web search; see references)

**(a) Hartle–Hawking–Hertog 2008** [1, 2]. In Planck units ($\hbar = c = G = 1$), the classical histories are a one-parameter family labeled by $\phi_0$, the modulus of the scalar at the South Pole of the complex "fuzzy instanton." Classicality at late times (their condition 3.13, $|\nabla I_R| \ll |\nabla S|$) requires $\phi_0 > \phi_0^c$, with $\phi_0^c \to 1.27$ as $\Lambda \to 0$, "independently of the value of $m^2$." The real part of the action is $I_R \approx -\pi/(2(H\mu\phi_R(0))^2)$, which they identify as "the action of Euclidean de Sitter space with effective cosmological constant $3m^2(\phi_R(0))^2$," and "without further constraints the NBWF universally favors histories with $\phi_0$ near the lower bound $\phi_0^c$." So the branch structure does produce a *family* of effective Hubble rates $H_{\rm eff}^2 \simeq \Lambda/3 + m^2\phi_0^2/6$, continuously labeled and bounded below by classicality. **Verdict: a modulus, not a hierarchy.** Every branch scale is $\ge \Lambda^{1/2}$ and is built from the input $m$; the weighting selects the *lowest* available scale, not an exponentially separated one. Nothing below the inputs is generated. **(Rigorous as a reading of [1].)**

**(b) Halliwell–Hawking 1985** [3]. The inhomogeneous modes start in their ground state (fixed by Euclidean regularity at the pole), stay there until horizon exit, and are amplified afterwards. The scales entering are $H$ (set by the scalar mass $m$, an input) and the comoving wavenumber $k/a$. **Verdict: no new scale**; what it contributes is the *state* (see §3, where this becomes an asset). **(Rigorous as a reading of the abstract; body not re-derived.)**

**(c) Coleman–Weinberg 1973** [4] is the origin of the term "dimensional transmutation": a massless scalar QED develops a vev at $\langle\phi\rangle \sim \mu\, e^{-(\ldots)/e^2}$. The March position's "Alvarez-Luna et al. 2023" **does exist**: Álvarez-Luna, de la Calle-Leal, Cembranos, Sanz-Cillero, JHEP 02 (2023) 232 [5]. What it shows (their eqs. 3.1, 3.21): starting from the scale-free Lagrangian $R^2/6f_0^2 + (\tfrac13 R^2 - R_{\mu\nu}^2)/f_2^2 + \tfrac12(\partial\phi)^2 - \tfrac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4 - \tfrac{\xi}{2}\phi^2 R$, graviton loops give $\phi$ a vev, $\bar M_{\rm Pl}^2 = \xi\langle\phi\rangle^2$, and the radial scalar mass is
$$ m_S^2 = \frac{\bar M_{\rm Pl}^2}{8\pi^2}\,\xi\left(9f_0^4\xi^2 + 4f_0^4 + 20 f_2^4\right). $$
The transmuted quantity is the vev, and $M_P$ is *built from* it; the scalar sits a *loop factor* below $M_P$, not an exponential. **Verdict: it answers "where does $M_P$ come from," not "why is $m \ll M_P$."** Making $m_S/M_P \sim 10^{-17}$ would need $\xi f^4 \sim 10^{-33}$, a tuning, not a transmutation. **(Rigorous as a reading of [5].)**

**(d) Anomaly-induced effective action** [6–9]. Riegert's nonlocal action reproduces the anomaly with a fourth-order conformally covariant operator; Antoniadis–Mottola show the conformal-factor theory has an infrared-stable fixed point with an *exact anomalous scaling dimension*; Barvinsky–Wachowski (2023) show the RG running "exhibits a kind of metamorphosis to the nonlocal form factors" $\log(\Box/\mu^2)$ of the cosmological and Einstein terms. In all of these, $a$ and $c$ appear as *coefficients of logarithms*, i.e. as $\beta$-function coefficients. The anomalous scaling at the Antoniadis–Mottola fixed point is a *power law* in scale with $O(1)$ exponents set by the field count. **Verdict: the anomaly coefficients alone cannot transmute.** A $\beta$-function coefficient is a "$b_0$," not a "$g^2$"; without a dimensionless coupling whose *initial value* is specified there is no $e^{-1/g^2}$. The one channel of this type that does exist, Fradkin–Tseytlin asymptotic freedom of the $C^2$ coupling [13, 14], needs $f_2^2(\mu_0)$ as input, and on a conformally flat cap ($C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} \equiv 0$ on the round $S^4$ and on de Sitter) that coupling does not even enter the saddle action, so the cap cannot weight it. **(Sketch.)**

**(e) No-boundary setups and mass hierarchies.** Three relevant things exist, none a hierarchy derivation. (i) Hawking–Hertog–Reall 2001 [15]: the no-boundary state with many conformal fields is nucleated by an $S^4$ instanton "much larger than the Planck scale provided there are sufficiently many matter fields," i.e. the repo's $H_0 \sim M_P/\sqrt{|a_2|}$ *is* a no-boundary result already in the literature, and it produces exactly that one scale. (ii) The Baum–Hawking argument [18] uses the weight $e^{3\pi/(G\Lambda)}$ to drive $\Lambda_{\rm eff} \to 0$; Duff [18] argues the proof is wrong. This is the only known case of the no-boundary weight *selecting* a scale-setting modulus, and it is disputed. (iii) Hartle–Hertog 2013 [19] obtain bounds on $\Lambda$ and $Q$ by combining the no-boundary prior with anthropic conditioning on a landscape, which is selection, not derivation. My searches found **no result deriving a particle-mass hierarchy from a no-boundary or tunneling geometry.** Reported as absence, not impossibility.

## 2. The commit: transmutation on the branch via the gauge trace anomaly

**Structural claim (Sketch).** On the framework's block the trace anomaly reads, schematically,
$$ \langle T^\mu{}_\mu\rangle \;=\; \underbrace{a E_4 + c\, C^2 + b\,\Box R}_{\text{curvature part: one-loop counting}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_i \frac{\beta_i(g_i)}{2 g_i}\,F_i^2}_{\text{gauge part: running coupling}} \;+\; (\text{mass terms}). $$
The curvature part is what `programs/co-emergence/index.tex` (Conjecture `conj:mass_generation`, eq. `eq:meff`) and `programs/fixed-point-existence/index.tex` (line 121, $H_0^2 = 180\pi/(G|a_2|)$, Sketch per `claims/fpe-starobinsky-coefficient.md`) already use: it fixes $H_0$ on the cap, and it fixes $m_{\rm eff}^2 = (\xi - \tfrac16)R \sim 12(\xi-\tfrac16)H_0^2$ on the branch. The gauge part is Collins–Duncan–Joglekar [10]; its physical content on the branch is Ji's decomposition [12], in which the nucleon mass $M_N = \langle N|T^\mu{}_\mu|N\rangle$ receives its chiral-limit value from the anomalous term $\frac{\beta}{2g}\langle F^2\rangle$. That term's scale is $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$, which at one loop is
$$ \Lambda_{\rm QCD} \;=\; \mu\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{8\pi^2}{b_0\, g^2(\mu)}\right), \qquad b_0 = 11 - \tfrac23 n_f \ \text{(SU(3))}. $$
So the *same object* that gives the framework its first scale on the cap gives it, on the branch, a second scale that is exponentially small in a dimensionless number. The two parts differ in exactly one respect: the curvature coefficients $a, c$ are pure counting (no coupling), while the gauge coefficient is $\beta(g)$, a function of a coupling. That difference is the whole content of the mass test.

**Which dimensionless number, and what the junction fixes (Sketch).** Write the transmutation in framework variables by choosing the matching scale to be the junction curvature. At the $K=0$ junction of a round cap of radius $a_0$, the intrinsic curvature is $^{(3)}R = 6/a_0^2 = 2\Lambda_{\rm eff}$, a covariant scalar, so $\mu_* \equiv a_0^{-1} = H_0$ is a legitimate, foliation-free matching scale. Then
$$ m_{\rm hadron} \;\sim\; \Lambda_{\rm QCD} \;=\; H_0\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{8\pi^2}{b_0\, g^2(H_0)}\right), \qquad H_0 \sim \frac{M_P}{\sqrt{|a_2|}}\ (\text{coefficient Sketch}). $$
The junction fixes: (i) $\mu_* = H_0$ (Rigorous, given the cap); (ii) $b_0$, once a gauge group and representations are specified, since $b_0$ is a sum over the same species list that produces $a$ and $c$ (Rigorous, standard). The junction does **not** fix (iii) $g^2(H_0)$. On the cap the gauge sector has no classical background; its coupling enters the saddle action only through instanton sectors, and at one loop the $k$-instanton weight at the cap scale is *exactly* the hierarchy raised to $b_0$:
$$ e^{-8\pi^2 |k|/g^2(H_0)} \;=\; \left(\frac{\Lambda_{\rm QCD}}{H_0}\right)^{b_0 |k|}. $$
**(Rigorous at one loop; it is the definition of $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$ raised to the power $b_0$.)** With the numbers below this is $\sim 10^{-120}$ per instanton. So the cap "knows" the hierarchy only as the exponential smallness of its own topological sectors: the no-boundary weight is flat in $g^2$ to that accuracy. **This is the sharpest form of the position: the coupling is a flat modulus of the root; the hierarchy is a property of the branch.** This is consistent with, and sharpens, the March synthesis's split between "mass gap origin" and "mass spectrum origin" (`explorations/2026-03-03-mass-gap-synthesis.md`, "The mass spectrum requires additional structure"): Conjecture 2 supplies the gap at $H_0$; transmutation supplies the spectrum's second scale; the two are different numbers from different parts of one anomaly.

**Worked order of magnitude.** Take $H_0 \sim 10^{-2.5} M_P \approx 4 \times 10^{16}$ GeV (the repo's $M_P/\sqrt{|a_2|}$ with $a_2 \sim 10^5$ in the paper's own convention; treat as order of magnitude only per `fpe-starobinsky-coefficient.md`) and $\Lambda_{\rm QCD} \approx 0.25$ GeV, $n_f = 6$, $b_0 = 7$:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| $\ln(H_0/\Lambda_{\rm QCD})$ | $\approx 39$ |
| Required $g^2(H_0) = 8\pi^2/(b_0 \cdot 39)$ | $\approx 0.29$ |
| Required $\alpha_s(H_0) = g^2/4\pi$ | $\approx 1/44$ |
| Same with $\mu_* = M_P$ ($\ln \approx 45$) | $\alpha_s(M_P) \approx 1/50$ |

This is the standard value of $\alpha_s$ near the GUT scale obtained by running the measured $\alpha_s(M_Z)$ upward (unverified by search this session; textbook), so the requirement is self-consistent with known physics: **the junction must supply a coupling of order $1/44$ for an SU(3)-like sector with $b_0 \approx 7$.** The exponent the junction must produce is a number of order 40.

**Could a junction quantity built from $(\Lambda, G, a, c)$ be that number? (Rigorous arithmetic, negative.)** With $\Lambda_{\rm eff} = 3H_0^2$ and $G = M_P^{-2}$:

| Candidate | Value | As exponent gives $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}/H_0 \sim$ |
|---|---|---|
| $G\Lambda_{\rm eff}$ (only dimensionless curvature invariant at $K=0$) | $\sim 3\times10^{-5}$ | $e^{-1/(G\Lambda)}$: absurd ($10^{-10^4}$) |
| $\ln[3\pi/(G\Lambda_{\rm eff})]$ (log of the no-boundary exponent) | $\approx 12.7$ | $e^{-12.7} \sim 10^{-5.5}$, off by $\sim 10^{12}$ |
| $3\pi/(G\Lambda_{\rm eff})$ used as $8\pi^2/g^2$ | $\approx 3\times10^5$ | $10^{-10^5}$: absurd |
| $1/a$, $1/c$ (SM: $a \approx 2.8$, $c \approx 2.4$) as $g^2$ | $\approx 0.36$–$0.42$ | $e^{-8\pi^2 a/b_0} \sim 10^{-13}$; within an order of magnitude in the exponent, but no mechanism and $a$, $b_0$ count different things |

None reproduces $\ln \approx 39$. The closest, $\ln(1/G\Lambda_{\rm eff}) \approx 13$, is short by a factor $\sim 3$ in the exponent, which is twelve orders of magnitude in the scale. I record the $1/a$ line as numerology and do not use it.

## 3. Axiom check

- **Smuggled background.** The running is in the renormalization scale $\mu$, and the covariant form of that running is the nonlocal form factor $\log(\Box/\mu^2)$ in the effective action [9], defined on the block without a foliation. The matching scale $\mu_* = H_0$ is a curvature invariant of the junction (Axiom `ax:nobackground` respected). What the argument *does* need is a state in which $\langle F^2\rangle$ is evaluated. Here precedent (b) turns into an asset: the no-boundary condition fixes the branch's mode functions in the Euclidean ground state [3], so the vacuum on the branch is fixed by cap regularity, not by an observer's choice. **(Sketch.)**
- **Preferred foliation or observer.** $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$ and $M_N = \langle N|T^\mu{}_\mu|N\rangle$ are scalars. The tree's partial order (the experimenter's rooted-tree orientation) is *not used* anywhere in the transmutation argument; the argument is agnostic to whether the FLT/Di Tucci–Lehners saddle dispute [16, 17] leaves the root unique.
- **Time sneaking in via running.** RG "time" $t = \ln\mu$ is not cosmic time, and the argument never uses the Born–Oppenheimer/WKB time that the intro of `index.tex` (line 126, citing Halliwell and Banks) criticizes. It needs only that the branch has proper length $\gg 1/\Lambda_{\rm QCD} \approx 10^{20} a_0$ for confinement to be a property of the branch at all. HHH's classicality condition guarantees long-lived classical branches for $\phi_0 > \phi_0^c$ [1], so this is satisfied without invoking a time parameter as input. The experimenter's willingness to reverse the Halliwell/Banks framing is therefore *orthogonal* to this attempt: it neither helps nor hurts it.
- **Hidden field-content assumption.** A non-abelian, asymptotically free sector is assumed. This is *not* new input relative to the paper: $a$ and $c$ are already sums over an assumed species list. But $b_0$ needs the group and representations, which $a, c$ do not; the framework derives neither. **(Rigorous as an accounting of assumptions.)**

## 4. What would kill this attempt

1. **A proof that the cap must weight $g^2$ nontrivially and that the weight selects $g^2 \to 0$ or $\to \infty$.** Then the branch's coupling would not be a flat modulus, and the hierarchy would be pushed to $0$ or $M_P$. Current evidence is the opposite: the $g$-dependence of the no-boundary weight is $O((\Lambda_{\rm QCD}a_0)^{b_0}) \sim 10^{-120}$, i.e. flat. A killing argument would have to come from a non-perturbative cap effect at scale $a_0^{-1} = H_0$, twenty orders above $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$, where the gauge sector is weakly coupled; I see no candidate.
2. **Failure of reflection positivity across the curved junction.** The state in which $\langle F^2\rangle$ is defined is supposed to come from Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction across the junction; `index.tex` (§ "Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction as flat-space evidence") notes the OS axioms as stated need a flat $E(4)$ background. If no reflection-positivity theorem holds on the round hemisphere with the $K=0$ equator, the transmuted scale has no Hilbert space to live in. (Reflection positivity across the equator of $S^d$ is, I believe, standard for conformal theories, but I have not verified a citation this session; see Unverified.)
3. **Branch too short.** If the classical branch could not sustain proper length $\gg 1/\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$, confinement would not be a branch property. HHH [1] rules this out for $\phi_0 > \phi_0^c$.
4. **A two-loop or threshold effect that destroys the $\mu_* = H_0$ matching.** This would change $g^2(H_0)$ by $O(10\%)$, not kill the mechanism; recorded for completeness.

None of these is a kill on present evidence. The attempt's honest weak point is not that it fails but that it *relocates* the question: from "where does the second scale come from" to "where does $g^2(H_0) \approx 4\pi/44$ come from."

## 5. If negative, the adaptation it points to

The precise second datum the junction must carry is **one dimensionless coupling of order $1/(4\pi) \times O(1)$ for a sector with a positive one-loop coefficient of order 10**, specified at $\mu_* = H_0$. Equivalently, one Euclidean instanton weight $e^{-8\pi^2/g^2(H_0)} \sim 10^{-120}$ on the cap. It would have to come from one of three places, in decreasing order of compatibility with the axioms:

1. **Level 0/1 (topology and smooth structure).** On $S^4$ the instanton number is $c_2$ of the bundle, a topological datum; the *coupling* is not. A Level-1 measure over smooth structures that induces an effective gauge coupling (the Asselmeyer-Maluga direction, Position 1 of the March debate, unverified per the synthesis) is the only route that keeps mass "purely geometric" (Axiom `ax:nobackground`, "matter emerges from topology (Conjecture)"). This is the adaptation I recommend pursuing: **a self-consistency condition at Level 1 that fixes $g^2(H_0)$, tested by whether it lands within a factor $\sim 2$ of $1/44$.** (Conjecture.)
2. **A Robin-type junction datum.** Di Tucci–Lehners [17] show the no-boundary saddle is rescued by Robin boundary conditions that "may be interpreted as an initial state with Euclidean momentum, with the quantum uncertainty shared between initial size and momentum." A gauge-field analogue, a Robin datum for $A_\mu$ at the junction, would be exactly a coupling-like boundary number carried by the junction rather than by the bulk. This is new and untested; it adds one junction datum beyond the conformal factor. (Conjecture.)
3. **Landscape weighting à la Hartle–Hertog [19].** Anthropic conditioning on $g^2$. This is selection, not derivation, and I recommend the program reject it as an answer while noting it is the *only* precedent for no-boundary selection of a modulus.

Whichever is chosen, the target is quantitative and falsifiable: produce $\ln(H_0/\Lambda) \approx 39 \pm 5$.

## 6. What is underspecified

- **$H_0$ itself.** The coefficient $180\pi/(G|a_2|)$ is Sketch with a recorded dimensional-convention defect and a possible factor $16\pi$ (`fpe-starobinsky-coefficient.md`); my estimate treats $H_0$ as $10^{-2}$–$10^{-3} M_P$ and the result is insensitive at the level of $\pm 3$ in a logarithm of 39.
- **$b_0$.** Needs a gauge group and representation content; the framework's $a, c$ counting does not supply them.
- **One loop only.** Threshold matching at $m_t$, $m_b$, $m_c$ and two-loop terms shift $\alpha_s(H_0)$ by $O(10\%)$; the position does not depend on this.
- **The state.** The identification of the branch vacuum with the Euclidean ground state through the junction assumes a reflection-positivity theorem on the curved junction that the repo does not have.
- **Two meanings of "mass."** Conjecture 2's $m_{\rm eff} \sim \sqrt{\xi}\,H_0$ (cap-side, conformal factor) and this attempt's $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$ (branch-side, transmuted) are both "mass"; the co-emergence chain needs only the former to activate, and the hierarchy needs the latter. The paper should say which it means where.
- **The signature-change junction's regularity.** `programs/signature-change-boundary/README.md` requires only a bounded proper expansion rate at $\Sigma$, weaker than $K=0$; the matching scale $\mu_* = a_0^{-1}$ used here assumes the round-cap $K=0$ junction. A junction with bounded but nonzero $\mathcal H$ would shift $\mu_*$ by $O(1)$, again invisible in a logarithm of 39.
