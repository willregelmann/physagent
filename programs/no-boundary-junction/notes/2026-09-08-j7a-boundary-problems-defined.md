## 1. Setting

The minisuperspace action of the J-1 note (Euler–Lagrange ≡ trace equation R = ½E₄ + ε□R, constant C = +8 fixed in J-6 §1.6) is fourth order in a(τ). Its Ostrogradsky phase space is (a, v, p_a, p_v) with p_v = ∂L/∂a″ and p_a = ∂L/∂v − dp_v/dτ; the Ostrogradsky energy vanishes on shell (`j7/ostro.py`: it equals (1 − C/8)/sin τ on the sphere, which is what fixes C = 8). A well-posed Dirichlet problem at the cut fixes two of the four; the two the record has been using are

- **(a, v) = (h_ij, K):** Dirichlet on the three-metric and its normal derivative — the Ostrogradsky-natural problem; position T's census and the whole regular-cap family are parametrized by it.
- **(q, R) = (h_ij, R):** Dirichlet on the three-metric and the four-dimensional Ricci scalar — the f(R)-natural problem, equivalent to fixing the scalaron φ = f′(R) in the scalar-tensor form (Dyer–Hinterbichler); position H's recontracting-cap search, and the representation position H named when it wrote "K = 0 for the (q, R) choice with its Legendre boundary term" (position H, §3).

The J-6 review (§9) said the two searches cannot be compared "as one amplitude." This note supplies the missing objects: the action of each problem and the transformation between them.

**The invariant action.** In the ESU frame the Riegert functional 2σ_ηη² + 8σ_η² + 8 gives p_v^{ESU} = (ε/4)a²R + (1 − v²)/2, but its cut action diverges logarithmically at the pole (∫8 dτ/a), the origin of the J-1 error. Steelman H's construction (J-6 §1.7) replaces it by the a-anomaly Wess–Zumino functional on the unit S⁴ in the conformal frame θ(τ) with dθ/dτ = sin θ/a, gauge-fixed by θ(cut) = π/2, plus HHJ's boundary term, and moves the Riegert scheme's local R² piece into the local part (the factor 3ε/(3ε+1)); it is finite, frame-independent to 10⁻⁸, and a functional of the cap alone. The question this note had to answer first is what boundary problem I_inv is the action *of* — frame invariance does not say.

## 2. Result 1 — I_inv is the fixed-(a, v) action; its momenta

**Analytic identification.** In the gauge θ(cut) = π/2, parametrize the cap by σ(θ) with the physical metric e^{2σ}(dθ² + sin²θ dΩ₃²) on [0, π/2]; then a = e^σ sin θ and v = σ_θ sin θ + cos θ, so at the cut a₁ = e^{σ₁} and v₁ = σ_θ(π/2). Every term of I_inv is a local functional of (σ, σ_θ, σ_θθ; θ) on this interval, so its variation at the cut has the Ostrogradsky form p_σ δσ + p_{σθ} δσ_θ, i.e. P δa + Q δv with no δa″; the pole variations vanish on regular caps (σ_θ(0) = 0, densities carrying sin³θ, local p_a = O(τ)); and the map cap ↦ σ(θ) on the fixed interval is a bijection (backward integration of dθ/dτ = sin θ/a from any C reaches θ = 0 at the pole), so the frame constant C* does not enter the variation. The v-momentum collects three pieces: from the R² term, (3ε+1)/2 · (1 − aa″ − v²) · 3ε/(3ε+1) = (ε/4)a²R; from Γ_KS, ∂/∂σ_θθ of (1/8)sin³θ(… + 8σ_θθσ_θ² …) = sin³θ₁ σ_θ² = v² at θ₁ = π/2; from HHJ's −σ_θ³/3, −v². The last two cancel identically in every frame (HHJ's Q₄ term carries no σ_θ). Hence

  **Q = ∂I_inv/∂v |_a = (ε/4) a² R,  P = ∂I_inv/∂a |_v = p_a^{ESU} = [−3a²v + 3εa²a‴ + 3εa v a″ − 6εv³ − 2v³ + 6εv + 6v]/(2a).**

P = p_a^{ESU} because I_inv and the ESU-frame action differ at the cut by F(v) = −v/2 + v³/6, with F_v = Q − p_v^{ESU} and F_a = 0. The attack pass re-derived both momenta off-shell in σ(θ) variables (p_σ^{KS} = 3v − v³ at π/2; p_a^{ESU} − p_a^{inv} = 0 identically), so the identification holds as a functional statement, not only on shell (`attack-j7a.md` §14).

## 3. Result 2 — the fixed-(q, R) action and the canonical relation

Given δI_inv = P δa + Q δv with Q = (ε/4)a²R, the action stationary at fixed (a, R) is the Legendre transform in (v, Q):

  **I_(a,R) = I_inv − v Q = I_inv − (ε/4) a² R v |_cut,  δI_(a,R) = [P − (ε/2) a R v] δa − (ε/4) a² v δR.**

No integral term is needed in the boundary function because Q has no v-dependence at fixed (a, R) (the general recipe is B = −vQ + ∫v ∂_vQ dv; for the ESU scheme it would have been −(ε/4)a²Rv − v/2 + v³/6, and the two agree after the F(v) shift, `j7/hj_check.py`). The a-momentum at fixed R is P − (ε/2)aRv, and the momentum conjugate to R is P_R = −(ε/4)a²v: it vanishes at every K = 0 cut — which is why the two representations coincide on reflection-symmetric cuts, why nothing in J-1 (closed caps, no cut) is affected, and, as §6 shows, why the K = 0 cut is the natural-boundary-condition saddle of this problem. At the flat ball (v = 1, R = 0) the boundary term vanishes but P_R = −(ε/4)a² does not.

This is the f(R) Gibbons–Hawking term. The R² piece of the action in the invariant scheme is β∫√g R² with β = −ε/(96π²) (per a); Dyer–Hinterbichler's rule (their eq. (5.16), S = ∫√g F(R) + 2∮√h F′(R)K, with "δR = 0 on the boundary as well") gives 4β∮√h R K = 24π² β a² R v = −(ε/4)a²Rv, exactly the numerically verified −vQ; the attack pass checked the Euclidean sign transfer and the GHY structure in this signature independently (`attack-j7a.md` §15). The Einstein–Hilbert GHY term is already inside I_EH, and the anomaly sector needs none at the (a, v) → (a, R) step because its Q vanishes. The two Dirichlet problems are therefore **(h_ij, K)** and **(h_ij, R) = (h_ij, φ)**, both well posed, related by the canonical transformation (v, Q) → (R, P_R) with generating function −(ε/4)a²Rv.

At the level of wavefunctions the relation is a transform in v,

  Ψ_∓(a, R) = ∫_𝒞 dv exp[±(ε/4)a²Rv] Ψ_∓(a, v),  Ψ_∓(a, v) ≈ Σ_saddles e^{∓I_inv},

upper sign Hartle–Hawking, so that the exponent is ∓(I_inv − vQ) = ∓I_(a,R) and the v-saddle condition Q(a, v) = (ε/4)a²R says the (a, v)-saddle has the prescribed R. **The canonical contour is v ∈ iℝ.** With τ = it, v = −i v_L for the real Lorentzian expansion rate v_L = da/dt, and Q_L = ∂S/∂v_L = Q; the passage from the configuration representation Ψ(a, v_L) to the mixed representation Ψ(a, Q_L) is the Fourier transform with kernel e^{−iQ_L v_L} = e^{+Qv}, a pure phase on the imaginary v-axis. Both surviving definitions of the state (H′ through Di Tucci–Lehners' Robin condition on a Lorentzian lapse integral, T′ through Feldbrugge–Lehners–Turok's real-lapse contour) are Lorentzian-contour definitions for which this is the default. The Euclidean-real-section contour v ∈ ℝ (real Euclidean cut data) is a different definition, examined in §5 because it is the one on which the recontracting cap lives.

## 8. Result 7 — the off-shell construction at the pole: conical initial data

The regular-cap family has no continuous off-shell direction at fixed boundary data, so a saddle-point "relevance" statement needs an off-shell integral. The natural enlargement is toward the cones a = vτ (v ≠ 1), approached through regular-pole geometries: a = τ + (v − 1)(√(τ² + δ²) − δ) (a(0) = 0, a′(0) = 1, a″(0) = (v − 1)/δ) or the fully regular odd family a = τ + (v − 1)τ³/(τ² + δ²) (a″(0) = 0, finite R at the pole). Both give I = c ln(1/δ) + O(1) with the same coefficient (sympy on the sharp cone, `j7/ostro.py`; quadrature at δ = 10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴ on both families, `j7/cone_num.py`, `stm_cone.out`, agreeing to three digits at ε = 0.25, 0.3, 1 and v = 0.1 … 2):

  c(v, ε) − c(1, ε) = −(v − 1)² [3ε(v + 1)² + (v − 1)(v + 3)] / (4v) = −3ε(v + 1)²(v − 1)²/(4v) − (v − 1)³(v + 3)/(4v),

the first term the R² sector (≤ 0 for every v > 0 and ε > 0: R ∝ (1 − v²)/τ² on the cone and a³R² ∝ 1/τ), the second the anomaly sector (> 0 for v < 1, < 0 for v > 1, a Casimir-like cost of a narrow cone). The bracket has the single positive root **v*(ε) = 2/√(1 + 3ε) − 1**: c(v) − c(1) < 0 (I → −∞) for v > v*(ε), v ≠ 1, and > 0 (I → +∞) for 0 < v < v*(ε); v* = 0.51, 0.45, 0.39, 0.26, 0.08, 0 at ε = 0.25, 0.3, 0.355, 0.5, 0.8, 1, and for ε ≥ 1 the coefficient is −(v − 1)²(v + 2) ≤ 0 for all v. The divergence lives in the cone region δ ≪ τ ≪ 1 and is blind to every datum at the pole. The coefficient is I_inv's, scheme-independently: the anomaly sector of I_inv (the KS functional on the unit S⁴; HHJ's term is cut-local and δ-independent) gives −(v − 1)³(v + 3)/(4v) on the cone (`atk_cone.out`), and ESU-anomaly − (1/288π²)∫R² − KS = 2 = c(1) identically. Hence:

- **No pole condition bounds either exponent.** The smoothed cones are regular-pole geometries, so the fourth-order action on regular-pole off-shell configurations is unbounded below near every cone with v > v*(ε) (Hartle–Hawking sign: in every neighbourhood of the regular pole, coefficient −3ε(v − 1)² on both sides of v = 1, and on every wide cone) and unbounded above near every needle cone with v < v*(ε) (tunneling sign, ε < 1). At the benchmark ε = 1 the tunneling sign is protected against every cone; inside H's window it is not. This is an asymmetry of *where* in cone angle each sign fails, not a one-sided protection: the R² sector is what enhances cones under e^{−I}, the anomaly sector what enhances needles under e^{+I}.
- **Either state must exclude the near-conical locus by its contour** — a Di Tucci–Lehners β_max-type refinement, mandatory here for both signs (steelman H's flaw 5 said this for the Hartle–Hawking sign; this extends it to the tunneling sign in the window). The regular-pole restriction defines the *saddle set*; it does not define the off-shell measure of either state.
- The codimension-2 statement in the literature is Fursaev–Solodukhin (1995): "although arbitrary curvature polynomials in such a procedure turn out to be divergent and depend on regularization, some specific integral quantities can be finite" — their eqs. (2.31)–(2.33) for ∫R², ∫Ric², ∫Riem² on C_α × Σ carry a divergent Y(α) and a regularization-dependent I₂(α). Our pole is a codimension-4 cone over S³, computed directly above; the analogy is structural, not a citation of their result for this case.

*Rigor:* Rigorous (sympy + numerical) in minisuperspace, scheme-independent; the extension to inhomogeneous conical data is not attempted.

## 9. What is now defined, what is not, and the adaptation

**Defined.** (i) The fixed-(a, v) action I_inv with its momenta; (ii) the fixed-(q, R) action I_inv − (ε/4)a²Rv with its momenta; (iii) the canonical transformation between them and its quantum form, canonically a Fourier transform over the real Lorentzian expansion rate; (iv) the saddle sets of each problem at the tested data with their invariant actions and, for the (a, v) census and the real-data complex caps, the constant-lapse test; (v) the two marginals: the v-marginal of Ψ(a, v), stationary on flat balls (Q = 0), and the R-marginal of Ψ(a, R), stationary on K = 0 cuts (P_R = 0); (vi) the behaviour of the action on conical initial data and on the real family in both representations.

**Not defined — the obstruction, restated after the passes.** One contour: the (a, v) problem's. §5 shows that the (q, R) problem reduces to it through a standard transform (the Euclidean-real-section alternative diverges under e^{−I} and is multi-branch under e^{+I}); §7 shows the (a, v) saddle set is ordered by neither sign even on the constant-lapse subset and that admissibility turns on the half-plane of the lapse; §8 shows the near-conical locus must be excluded by the contour for both signs; §6 shows the real-family runaway is not a discriminator and relocates between representations. FLT's real-lapse contour and DTL's Robin condition were formulated for second-order gravity (J-6 §1.8–1.9); the record contains no lapse contour for the fourth-order theory, and without it neither "relevance" nor a sign can be stated.

**The adaptation** (per the standing instruction that an obstruction names its adaptation). Construct the fourth-order lapse integral: the minisuperspace path integral over a(t) with lapse N in Ostrogradsky variables (a, v, p_a, p_v), the (a, v) data at the cut, and the constraint. Then J-7b should: (1) determine whether FLT's real-N contour or a DTL-type Euclidean-momentum condition extends to it — this fixes the sign, the half-plane of N_E (hence whether saddle A of §7 and the constant-lapse pair of §5 are admitted), and the (a, v) thimbles; (2) impose the near-conical exclusion of §8 as part of that contour, for whichever sign results; (3) evaluate the canonical transform to (q, R) along v ∈ iℝ, for which the census at (3, −i√8) is already one point of the integrand, and check the K = 0 selection of §6 against relevance; (4) repair the six census gauges and complete the constant-lapse census; (5) test ε = 0.3 and ε = 1 in each step. Nothing in this note requires choosing between H′ and T′ first.
