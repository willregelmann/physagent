## Executive summary

**No saddle prescription is selected: dominance has not been established for either candidate definition.** The two paths require related but distinct boundary-value problems. Review on September 8, 2026 narrowed three conclusions of the original synthesis: a common thimble computation was not specified, FLT's bound does not prove a bounded wavefunction, and the searches do not establish a complete discrete classical ensemble (§9).

- **Path H (Hartle–Hawking sign).** The round-sphere negative-mode condition gives ε > 1/4; combining it with J-1's comparison of the two known reflection-symmetric instantons gives the conditional window **1/4 < ε < ε_c ≈ 0.355**. Within the tested exactly-real closed continuations, the sphere is the only large expanding history found in this window. The inherited seed estimate gives ≈ 3.7–5 e-folds at a₂ = 10⁶; a complete HHH classicality analysis, including the histories that exit, is outstanding. Above ε_c the double bubble has lower action than the sphere and has both a closed recollapsing and an open super-inflating continuation (§1.10). At fixed **(q₁, R₁) = (4, 12)** a real recontracting cap has lower reported invariant action than the sphere by 5–31a; relevance and the variational boundary action in this representation must be established before this becomes an amplitude-dominance result. Flat balls belong to a separately defined radius marginal. *(Window: Sketch conditional on the restricted ensemble and relevance; saddle observations: Rigorous (numerical), within the searches.)*
- **Path T (tunneling sign).** The candidate is a hybrid: real-lapse background plus Vilenkin–Yamada field boundary condition, whose tensor extension remains open. Conditional on a real-action defining contour, FLT excludes exponentially enhanced relevant saddle factors; **boundedness of the full wavefunction is unproved**. The census at fixed **(a₁, v₁) = (3, −i√8)**, where v = da/dτ, found the sphere and at least eighteen other complex caps. The sphere was the only exactly-real Lorentzian continuation identified in that search; a nearly classical candidate P has less suppression in the old J-1 action scheme, and its invariant ranking and relevance remain unknown. The two-known-instanton ordering reverses under the tunneling sign; this alone is not a proof of dominance in the full history measure. *(Numerical observations at ε = 1; sign and history interpretation conditional.)*
- **Costs of the definitions.** H adds a background Robin condition and seeks a regular field state; T adds a field condition and seeks a suppressed background saddle. Neither definition has a completed fourth-order off-shell construction here. Neither global boundedness nor an exhaustive classical ensemble is available as a discriminator.
- **J-7: specify the integrals, then compute relevance.** Separate the fixed-(a,v) census, the fixed-(q,R) recontracting-cap problem, and the radius marginal. Derive the variational boundary terms and any canonical transform, including the anomaly boundary functional; specify the integration cycles and off-shell variables. Then attempt the corresponding thimble decompositions and rank only saddles of the same integral. ε = 1 is a benchmark outside H's proposed window; an H-window test requires, for example, ε = 0.3. A result at one ε or an obstruction need not select a sign.

**Sign-independent results the debate established** (§1), several of which correct the record: the J-1 anomaly-action correction (merged, #222); the flat 4-ball as a regular saddle with local action −(3/4)a q₁ and the real family's action unbounded below for ε > 0 (three independent computations); the ε = 1/4 negative-mode threshold; the double bubble's open history; the HHJ boundary terms; the constant that makes the fixed-lapse Lagrangian consistent; the tensor-form positivity; the Robin condition's blindness to the cap; the sign as the orientation of the 4-volume form rather than of time.

## 2. Path H — surviving form

**H′.** The no-boundary state is the sum over regular caps with weight e^{−I}, implemented by the Robin / Euclidean-momentum definition; the comparison below is restricted to the known exactly-real continuations, using the HHH leading semiclassical weighting, weight e^{−2I_R} with I_R the frame-invariant half-action at the junction.

1. *(Rigorous bookkeeping; interpretation as in §1.9)* The sign is the orientation of the Euclidean four-volume form; both caps satisfy the Robin condition.
2. *(Rigorous by citation + derived map; Sketch for the window)* On the round sphere under e^{−I}: tensors Gaussian, scalars Gaussian except the homogeneous mode, iff ε > 1/4. **Window 1/4 < ε < ε_c ≈ 0.355, i.e. 1.68 H₀ < M < 2.00 H₀; N_inf ≈ 3.7–5.0 (a₂ = 10⁶), 4.9–6.5 (10⁷)** on the inherited seed estimate. Below 1/4 the sphere is a multi-negative-mode saddle of the HH state; neither sign has a Gaussian sphere there in the O(5) analysis.
3. *(Sketch, restricted ensemble)* In the window, the sphere is the only large expanding closed history found among the tested exactly-real continuations. Above ε_c the double bubble's two known histories outweigh the sphere under the assumed e^{−ΔI} rule. The window and depth estimate are conditional on this restricted comparison and on saddle relevance. **The claim that the full classical ensemble is discrete is withdrawn:** absolute Re I_inv drift on a few complex continuations does not evaluate HHH's componentwise relative-gradient criterion or prove completeness. The scalaron seed that supplies a decaying branch still needs a classical-history construction.
4. *(Numerical action comparison; amplitude dominance Conjecture)* The fixed-(q₁,R₁) recontracting solution has lower reported invariant cut action than the sphere (§1.4). To use it in Ψ(q₁,R₁), J-7 must first check the fixed-R variational boundary action and then relevance. It is absent from the fixed-(a₁,v₁) census by its different final derivative, not by a thimble exclusion. The flat-ball comparison concerns a radius marginal whose measure and boundary condition must also be defined. Regularity alone does not settle any of these relevance questions.
5. Conditioning on a large universe (Hawking–Hertog 2006; Hartle–Hawking–Hertog 2008, verified) removes the double bubble's closed recollapsing branch above a_peak² (not its open history), at the price e^{ΔI·a} in unconditioned probability; H′ commits to the unconditioned measure on the reframing's own premise (the root explains the initial state). *(Judgment, stated as such.)*

What H′ lost under attack: "no lapse saddle / PL inapplicable" (false — flat space is one; PL is a two-variable problem); the wrong Lagrangian in the lapse setup (§1.8); "regularity as a property of the contour" (DTL's off-shell geometries do not start at zero size; regularity is a saddle outcome); "neither continuation is a root" (the open branch inflates); the "matter boundary state" (§1.7); the neck-data Newton search (three real saddles at the exact data; the double bubble and its asymmetric neighbour degenerate to 10⁻⁴a in the invariant action — a fold, which makes its one-loop prefactor singular).

## 3. Path T — surviving form

**T′.** The no-boundary state is the real-lapse Lorentzian path integral with the fourth-order theory's second pole datum supplied by regularity (the sum over regular caps) and the field state supplied by Vilenkin–Yamada's boundary condition at a → 0 (parameter-free, h_n = −in/a).

1. *(Rigorous, verbatim FLT; conditional on inheritance by the regular-cap sum)* "Since the real part of the semiclassical exponent starts out zero on the real axis, it must be negative at any relevant saddle point." Every relevant saddle has semiclassical exponential magnitude ≤ 1; the HH-signed sphere is excluded, not disfavored. The fork is "tunneling sphere or no sphere" versus "Robin term with a tuned window."
2. *(Rigorous (numerical), ε = 1)* In the tested fixed-(a,v) census, the sphere is the only saddle identified with an exactly-real Lorentzian continuation; four complex saddles are less suppressed than it (by up to e^{4.1a}, J-1 scheme) and persist across ε; one, P, is nearly classical (Re I nearly frozen, momentum nearly real, R → 11.3) and less suppressed by e^{1.5a} at a₁ = 4 — unranked in a frame-invariant scheme. **Dominance and completeness remain open; the exactly-real observation is limited to the tested census.**
3. *(Sketch, conditional)* In the two-known-instanton comparison, the sphere's history beats the double bubble's by e^{|ΔI|} under the tunneling sign. That comparison supplies no J-1 upper bound; the inherited N_inf ≈ 60 estimate at ε ≈ 5 is not excluded by it. Full-ensemble dominance and an exiting classical history remain unproved.
4. *(Conjecture — not established)* Boundedness of the tunneling wavefunction does not follow from item 1. A saddle expansion includes fluctuation determinants, multiplicities, and possibly infinitely many terms; none is uniformly controlled here. The surviving conditional theorem is only absence of exponentially enhanced relevant saddle factors. Likewise, the HH real-family runaway is an action observation, not a proof that the defined wavefunction diverges without a relevance and summation analysis.
5. *(Conceded)* T′ is a hybrid: on the tunneling sheet the tensor form is −F < 0, no Euclidean vacuum exists there, and VY's boundary term re-flips the modes by hand, selecting mode functions that diverge at the pole (VY's own text; FLT App. B: "tends to infinity as τ^{−k−1}"). The tensor-sector extension is unconstructed and contested (FLT 2018 vs VY-II 2019, exact for a conformal scalar, this program's matter). Under T′ the cap's Euclidean vacuum is an *input* to J-4, not an output.
6. *(Rigorous identity; Sketch reading)* I(T) even in T; the sign is the orientation of the 4-volume form; no arrow from the contour.
7. *(Sketch)* The negative-mode window 0 < ε < 1/4 is harmless for T′ (those modes are suppressed on the tunneling sheet) and problematic for H′.

What T′ lost under attack: "definitional economy"; "sphere unique dominant at every ε"; "HH has no dominant saddle" as a one-sided argument (the complex set grows both ways; only the real family is one-sided); the Casimir-term bookkeeping (§1.8); "the runaway is monotone" (the maximum is at flat space); a mixed Planck-mass convention; the §3.4 sheet-assignment Sketch (replaced by the FLT bound plus the 2D problem); the "J-4 gains a premise" row (backwards).

## 4. Adversarial critique across the survivors, and what converged

These are genuine independent confirmations, not a shared premise: the two positions used different gauges, different reference frames for the anomaly action, and different search strategies, and the attacks ran their own.

**What the comparison can establish.** H's reported recontracting competitor belongs to a fixed-(q,R) shooting problem; T's P belongs to fixed-(a,v). They motivate related relevance calculations, but are not competitors in one shared amplitude. H's field-state argument and T's conditional exponential suppression remain reasons to investigate each definition. A rigorous boundedness asymmetry and an exhaustive classical-ensemble comparison have not been established.

**J-1 remains conditional.** Its two-instanton ordering depends on the sign. Turning that ordering into a coupling bound further requires relevance, a specified history ensemble, and the inherited seed assumption. J-7 can constrain these choices; it is not guaranteed to select a sign.

## 9. Review corrections — September 8, 2026

The PR #221 review and independent defense upheld three gaps. This revision updates the retained synthesis, README and OBJECTIVES; the six dated debate files remain historical evidence and are not silently rewritten.

1. **Boundary data.** The T scripts use K for v = da/dτ, whereas H often uses K = v/a; neither is interchangeable with R at fixed a. In the archived `steelmanH/invariant.out`, q = 4, R = 12 gives v/a = −0.8660i for the sphere and −1.7992 for the recontracting cap. The latter cannot occur at the former's fixed v. A change of representation needs a derived boundary action; HHJ frame invariance does not replace a variational-principle check.
2. **Exponent versus wavefunction.** [Feldbrugge–Lehners–Turok, Lorentzian Quantum Cosmology (2017), §II](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.02076) establishes suppression of individual semiclassical exponential factors when Re(iS) starts at zero on the defining cycle. It does not bound determinants or an infinite saddle sum. This affirms the real-contour suppression argument against an enhanced HH saddle under that same definition; it does not decide the distinct Robin definition. Global boundedness is open.
3. **Classicality and completeness.** [Hartle–Hawking–Hertog (2008), §III.2 and Appendix A.4](https://arxiv.org/html/0803.1663) requires |∇_A I_R| ≪ |∇_A S| componentwise. Real classical histories are extracted from S and need not coincide with the complex extrema. The archived tests of absolute drift do not establish an exhaustive discrete ensemble. Exactly-real solutions remain useful tested cases; the stronger exclusion is withdrawn.

Both sources were checked at full-text passage level in the review; these references remain exploratory-tier Markdown citations. No new numerical investigation is claimed by this correction. Revision by Codex at the experimenter's direction; the original debate provenance above is unchanged.
