## Executive summary

**No commitment is made, and the reason is a result, not a preference: neither sign currently defines a state in which the round S⁴ is the dominant saddle at its own data by computation, and both paths hinge on the same undone calculation.** Each path was built to its strongest form and attacked; each survived only narrowed, and in a form that mirrors the other:

- **Path H (Hartle–Hawking sign).** Survives as a statement in the Hartle–Hawking–Hertog measure on exactly real classical histories, with a window narrowed from both sides: **1/4 < ε < ε_c ≈ 0.355** (the lower edge is new — below it the sphere has inhomogeneous scalar negative modes under e^{−I}, from Hawking–Hertog–Reall's own spectrum; the upper edge is J-1's). In that window the sphere's de Sitter history is the unique closed classical history at large scale factor, with a decaying branch of ≈ 3.7–5 e-folds. Above ε_c the double bubble's histories dominate, and there are two: the closed recollapse J-1 found, and an open Hawking–Turok-type super-inflating history through the pole with N_open ≈ 1.6, 4.6, 24, 47, 95 e-folds at ε = 0.36, 1, 5, 10, 20, ending in a finite-time singularity with no exit. But as a statement that the sphere dominates the *amplitude* Ψ at a large three-sphere, path H is false unless the Robin-regularized thimble excludes two things no known criterion excludes: the flat ball and its real-cap neighbours (local action unbounded below along real regular caps, the fourth-order conformal-factor problem) and a real-Euclidean recontracting cap at the sphere's own data with action lower by 5–31a. That exclusion is path H's Conjecture. *(Window: Sketch; the negative-mode threshold Rigorous by citation with the map α_HHR = −ε/2 derived three ways; the saddle facts Rigorous (numerical).)*
- **Path T (tunneling sign).** Survives only as a hybrid, T′: real-lapse contour for the background plus Vilenkin–Yamada's boundary condition for the fields — because on the tunneling sheet the tensor quadratic form flips sign and no Euclidean vacuum exists there, so the field state must be put in by hand; "definitional economy" is dead, and the theory does not evade FLT's perturbation problem (the tensor form is positive and *steeper* than Einstein's at every mode). T′ has one theorem H lacks — on the real-lapse contour every relevant saddle has weight ≤ 1, so nothing is enhanced and the tunneling wave function is bounded on real minisuperspace — and one loss: at the de Sitter data the sphere is the unique exactly classical saddle but not the unique or the least-suppressed one; at least eighteen complex regular caps sit there, four less suppressed than the sphere by up to e^{4a}, and one nearly classical "fuzzy" saddle on de Sitter's stable manifold beats it by e^{1.5a} at a₁ = 4 unless the relevance analysis removes it. The J-1 bound is lifted in the history measure under T′; the R² coefficient is again a free modulus and sixty e-folds at ε ≈ 5 are allowed. *(Bound on relevant saddles: Rigorous, verbatim FLT, conditional on the regular-cap sum inheriting it; saddle facts Rigorous (numerical) at ε = 1; hybrid: conceded.)*
- **The honest comparison that replaces "which sign is right":** each path adds exactly one boundary term and derives the other sector for free. H fixes the background (Robin term, a tuned window β_min < |β| < β_max) and gets the field state — the Euclidean vacuum with Gaussian perturbations in the window — as output, but its background dominance is conjectural. T′ fixes the fields (Vilenkin–Yamada, parameter-free, contested by FLT for the tensor sector) and gets background boundedness for free, but its dominant saddle is open. **The program must decide which sector it wants derived; that decision is not made here.**
- **One computation decides both:** the two-dimensional Picard–Lefschetz descent flow over the lapse and the pole datum (R₀ = −72c₃) at the de Sitter data, as a function of a₁ along the history, with the Herzog–Huang–Jensen boundary terms that make cut-cap actions frame-independent so survivors can be ranked. It answers, for H, whether the Robin thimble through the sphere avoids the recontracting caps; for T′, whether the sphere's tunneling-sheet thimble is relevant at all and whether the fuzzy saddle is. It is well posed in minisuperspace, has not been done here or in the literature found, and is proposed as milestone J-7.

**Sign-independent results the debate established** (§1), several of which correct the record: the J-1 anomaly-action correction (merged, #222); the flat 4-ball as a regular saddle with local action −(3/4)a q₁ and the real family's action unbounded below for ε > 0 (three independent computations); the ε = 1/4 negative-mode threshold; the double bubble's open history; the HHJ boundary terms; the constant that makes the fixed-lapse Lagrangian consistent; the tensor-form positivity; the Robin condition's blindness to the cap; the sign as the orientation of the 4-volume form rather than of time.

**Orientation (the milestone's original content)** is unchanged from the #216 finding and is now shared by both paths: the tree re-expresses the branch's causal order (Σ as initial boundary) and the state's record arrow; it derives neither; and root uniqueness for *our* history holds on both paths — the sphere is the unique exactly classical saddle at its own data — while what differs between paths and remains open is probability.

## 2. Path H — surviving form

**H′.** The no-boundary state is the sum over regular caps with weight e^{−I}, implemented by the Robin / Euclidean-momentum definition; dominance means the Hartle–Hawking–Hertog measure on exactly real classical histories, weight e^{−2I_R} with I_R the frame-invariant half-action at the junction.

1. *(Rigorous, bookkeeping)* The sign is the orientation of Euclidean time at the pole; both caps satisfy the Robin condition.
2. *(Rigorous by citation + derived map; Sketch for the window)* On the round sphere under e^{−I}: tensors Gaussian, scalars Gaussian except the homogeneous mode, iff ε > 1/4. **Window 1/4 < ε < ε_c ≈ 0.355, i.e. 1.68 H₀ < M < 2.00 H₀; N_inf ≈ 3.7–5.0 (a₂ = 10⁶), 4.9–6.5 (10⁷)** on the inherited seed estimate. Below 1/4 the sphere is a multi-negative-mode saddle of the HH state; neither sign has a Gaussian sphere there in the O(5) analysis.
3. *(Sketch by citation; numbers Rigorous (numerical))* In the window the sphere's de Sitter history is the unique closed classical history at large scale factor. Above ε_c the double bubble's two histories (closed recollapse; open super-inflation, §1.10) dominate by e^{−ΔI} with the corrected ΔI. **The bound restated: a unique closed-universe root with a decaying branch exists iff 1/4 < ε < ε_c.** Nearly-real complex continuations are not classical at large scale factor (Re I_inv drifts by O(1)a within two e-folds), so the classical ensemble is discrete.
4. *(Rigorous (numerical) on the saddle set; relevance open — the path's Conjecture)* At the sphere's own data Ψ(q₁, R₁) under e^{−I} is dominated by the non-classical recontracting cap (§1.4) by e^{4.9a}–e^{31a}, and the R₁-marginal by flat balls (§1.2). The HH-sign state's classical predictions are a subdominant part of its amplitude at every large three-sphere — the fourth-order conformal-factor problem — unless the Robin thimble excludes those caps. DTL's β_max criterion (avoid singular geometries) does not remove them: they are non-singular. **This is the discriminator path T does not face.**
5. Conditioning on a large universe (Hawking–Hertog 2006; Hartle–Hawking–Hertog 2008, verified) removes the double bubble above a_peak² at every ε, at the price e^{ΔI·a} in unconditioned probability; H′ commits to the unconditioned measure on the reframing's own premise (the root explains the initial state). *(Judgment, stated as such.)*

What H′ lost under attack: "no lapse saddle / PL inapplicable" (false — flat space is one; PL is a two-variable problem); the wrong Lagrangian in the lapse setup (§1.8); "regularity as a property of the contour" (DTL's off-shell geometries do not start at zero size; regularity is a saddle outcome); "neither continuation is a root" (the open branch inflates); the "matter boundary state" (§1.7); the neck-data Newton search (three real saddles at the exact data; the double bubble and its asymmetric neighbour degenerate to 10⁻⁴a in the invariant action — a fold, which makes its one-loop prefactor singular).

## 3. Path T — surviving form

**T′.** The no-boundary state is the real-lapse Lorentzian path integral with the fourth-order theory's second pole datum supplied by regularity (the sum over regular caps) and the field state supplied by Vilenkin–Yamada's boundary condition at a → 0 (parameter-free, h_n = −in/a).

1. *(Rigorous, verbatim FLT; conditional on inheritance by the regular-cap sum)* "Since the real part of the semiclassical exponent starts out zero on the real axis, it must be negative at any relevant saddle point." Every relevant saddle has weight ≤ 1; the HH-signed sphere is excluded, not disfavored. The fork is "tunneling sphere or no sphere" versus "Robin term with a tuned window."
2. *(Rigorous (numerical), ε = 1)* The sphere is the unique exactly classical saddle at the de Sitter data and the unique one with real Ostrogradsky data; four complex saddles are less suppressed than it (by up to e^{4.1a}, J-1 scheme) and persist across ε; one, P, is nearly classical (Re I nearly frozen, momentum nearly real, R → 11.3) and less suppressed by e^{1.5a} at a₁ = 4 — unranked in a frame-invariant scheme. **"Sphere unique dominant" does not survive; "sphere unique classical, dominance among relevant saddles open" does.**
3. *(Sketch, conditional)* In the history measure the sphere's history beats the double bubble's by e^{|ΔI|} at every ε > ε_c; J-1's bound is lifted there; the R² coefficient is a free modulus; N_inf ≈ 60 at ε ≈ 5 is allowed.
4. *(Rigorous given item 1)* The tunneling wave function is bounded on real minisuperspace; the HH one is unbounded along the real regular caps (§1.2) and, if relevant, along the +1-sheet complex saddles (Re I to −59.5, no floor found). This asymmetry is real and is T′'s best structural point.
5. *(Conceded)* T′ is a hybrid: on the tunneling sheet the tensor form is −F < 0, no Euclidean vacuum exists there, and VY's boundary term re-flips the modes by hand, selecting mode functions that diverge at the pole (VY's own text; FLT App. B: "tends to infinity as τ^{−k−1}"). 6. *(Rigorous identity; Sketch reading)* I(T) even in T; the sign is the orientation of the 4-volume form; no arrow from the contour.
7. *(Sketch)* The negative-mode window 0 < ε < 1/4 is harmless for T′ (those modes are suppressed on the tunneling sheet) and problematic for H′.

What T′ lost under attack: "definitional economy"; "sphere unique dominant at every ε"; "HH has no dominant saddle" as a one-sided argument (the complex set grows both ways; only the real family is one-sided); the Casimir-term bookkeeping (§1.8); "the runaway is monotone" (the maximum is at flat space); a mixed Planck-mass convention; the §3.4 sheet-assignment Sketch (replaced by the FLT bound plus the 2D problem); the "J-4 gains a premise" row (backwards).

## 4. Adversarial critique across the survivors, and what converged

**Shared blind spot, caught.** Both positions computed with the lead's J-1 lapse-gauge Lagrangian in its wrong regularization for parts of their work (position H throughout its lapse setup; position T in its numerical actions), and both were caught by the attacks; the structural conclusions that survive are those shown to be scheme-independent (existence, thresholds, evenness, the runaway's sign, the flat ball, the classicality tests). Every quoted number at Lorentzian data in the two position files should be read as J-1-scheme unless the invariant is stated.

**Convergence, and whether it is real.** The two paths converge on: the sphere as the unique exactly classical saddle at its own data (found by both sides with different codes); the flat ball and the real family's unboundedness (three codes); the ε = 1/4 threshold (four derivations of the map); the Robin condition's blindness to the cap; the sign as a volume-form orientation. These are genuine independent confirmations, not a shared premise: the two positions used different gauges, different reference frames for the anomaly action, and different search strategies, and the attacks ran their own.

**Where the paths are mirror images.** H′'s adversary is a non-classical Euclidean cap with lower action at the sphere's data (real, non-singular, constant-lapse) whose relevance under the Robin thimble is unknown; T′'s adversary is a nearly classical complex cap with less suppression whose relevance under the real-lapse thimble is unknown. Each path's boundedness advantage is the other's problem sector: H′ is bounded in the fields and unbounded in the background; T′ is bounded in the background and has its field sector put in by hand. Neither "wins" a comparison at the level of computation available.

**What does not converge and must not be papered over.** Under H′, the junction constrains the □R coefficient (window 1/4 < ε < ε_c) and the root is shallow; under T′ it does not, and a sixty-e-fold root is allowed. J-1's result is therefore *conditional on the sign*, and the sign is conditional on J-7. The record should carry it that way.

What multiplicity affects is *probability*, and that is exactly the sign-dependent, J-7-conditional content of J-1.
