## 1. The principled case (Sketch; every quotation verified this session from the ar5iv/arXiv renderings)

**1.1 What FLT establish.** Feldbrugge, Lehners and Turok (PRL 119, 171301 (2017); PRD 95, 103508 (2017)) show, for Einstein–Λ minisuperspace in the gauge ds² = −N²dt²/q + q dΩ₃², that after the (exactly Gaussian) q-integral the lapse integral over real N is the whole content of the state, and that:

- "Therefore, in any meaningful semiclassical Euclidean path integral, one cannot integrate N_E over all real values." / "The Lorentzian path integral for quantum cosmology with a positive cosmological constant is meaningful in this approach, but the Euclidean version is not." (PRL, verbatim.)
- "Thus we revert to the Lorentzian path integral which, taken either over real 0⁺ < N < ∞, or −∞ < N < ∞, yields a conditionally convergent, meaningful result." / "Picard-Lefschetz theory allows us to render the integral absolutely convergent by distorting the N contour into the complex N-plane." (PRL, verbatim.)
- "We show that this procedure unambiguously determines which semiclassical saddle point solutions are relevant to the quantum mechanical amplitude. Imposing 'no-boundary' initial conditions, i.e., restricting attention to regular, complex metrics with no initial boundary, we find that the dominant saddle contributes a semiclassical exponential factor which is precisely the inverse of the famous Hartle-Hawking result." (PRD abstract, verbatim.)
- The result is the tunneling weight: for Λ = 3, q₁ = 9 the four saddles N_s = (3/Λ)(±i ± √(Λq₁/3 − 1)) carry Re(iS) = ∓4π² = ∓12π²/Λ (my `fltcheck.py` reproduces this from their S⁽⁰⁾(N)); the real contour picks Im N_s > 0, i.e. e^{−12π²/Λ}.

FLT 2018 (PRD 97, 023509, "No rescue for the no boundary proposal") then "prove there is no choice of complex contour for the lapse which avoids this problem" and that the Diaz Dorronsoro et al. contour "yields extra, non-perturbative corrections which again render the perturbations unsuppressed" (abstract, verbatim). Di Tucci–Lehners (PRD 98, 103506 (2018)) confirm the instability "even in this restricted context" of "a sum over purely regular geometries" (abstract, verbatim). So on the Lorentzian side the record is: real lapse ⇒ tunneling sign, and *with FLT's regularity condition on the modes* ⇒ inverse-Gaussian perturbations, for any contour and for regular geometries too.

**1.2 What the Robin redefinition gives up.** Di Tucci–Lehners (PRL 122, 201302 (2019)) obtain the Hartle–Hawking sign by adding the boundary term αq₀ + q₀²/(2β) at the initial surface and choosing α = −6π²i, and say so: "the present implementation of the no-boundary proposal effectively corresponds to a redefinition … the sum is redefined to be over geometries with approximately zero initial size and approximately Euclidean initial momentum" (verified in the J-6 draft this cycle; the polarity — Robin ⇒ HH sign, stable fluctuations — was verified in the #216 cycle). Their 2019 follow-up with Sberna (PRD 100, 123543) concludes that "all current working examples of the no-boundary wavefunction force one to abandon the notion of a sum over compact and regular geometries, and point to the importance of an initial Euclidean momentum" (abstract, verbatim). The price is explicit: the state is no longer *defined* by "no boundary"; it is defined by a chosen initial momentum with a chosen imaginary part, and the tunneling/HH dichotomy is moved into that choice.

**1.3 Why T is the right commitment for this program.** Three reasons, in order of weight.

(i) *Definitional economy.* The framework's block is a fixed four-geometry with no time; the object this program glues to it is a Lorentzian history. Position T defines the amplitude by the one integral that is convergent without a choice — the real-lapse integral — and lets the mathematics pick the saddle. Position H imports a parameter (β, the width of the initial coherent state) and a sign (Im α) whose only justification is the answer they produce.

(ii) *The unstable-sign theory has no Hartle–Hawking minimum.* §3.3 shows that for ε > 0 the Euclidean action of regular caps is unbounded below along a real direction. Under e^{−I} the no-boundary measure has no dominant saddle; the double bubble is not the answer, the runaway is. Under e^{+I} the sphere is the maximum-weight cap at its own data and the runaway is exponentially suppressed. J-1's ordering was a two-cap comparison; the full family makes the HH sign untenable *for this theory* independently of taste.

(iii) *The perturbation problem has a tunneling-side answer that fits the program's own premise.* Vilenkin–Yamada (PRD 98, 066003 (2018)) show that "the superspace approach requires Robin boundary conditions for the scalar field modes, the path integral approach requires adding an appropriate boundary term to the scalar field action, and the initial universe approach requires the initial quantum state of the scalar field to be Euclidean vacuum. We find that all three approaches yield identical wave functions and that scalar field fluctuations are well behaved, contrary to earlier claims in the literature" (abstract, verbatim). The condition "fields on the cap in the Euclidean vacuum" is exactly the premise of J-4 (reflection positivity of the Euclidean vacuum of free conformal fields on the round S⁴). Position T therefore does not *assume* the cap's field state; the mode boundary condition *is* that state. Both sides of the debate add a boundary term; T adds it to the fields (where the program already needed it), H adds it to the background (where the program's block has no time to give an initial momentum to).

**Where (iii) is contested.** FLT (Universe 4, 100 (2018), App. B) reply: "Vilenkin and Yamada propose to add a boundary term to the action, which has precisely the effect of cancelling the divergence of the action of the c₂ mode, while rendering that of the c₁ mode infinite," and "The perturbative terms scale as τ^{−2k−4} near a = 0. Thus at the 'bottom' of the instanton the solutions become entirely untrustworthy … the theory is out of control" (verbatim). Vilenkin–Yamada II (PRD 99, 066010 (2019)) answer with an exact solution: "The tunneling wave function of the universe is calculated exactly for a de Sitter minisuperspace model with a massless conformally coupled scalar field, both by solving the Wheeler-DeWitt equation and by evaluating the Lorentzian path integral. … The back-reaction of quantum field fluctuations on the scale factor amounts to a constant renormalization of the vacuum energy density. This is in contrast to the recent suggestion of Feldbrugge et al. that the back-reaction should diverge when the scale factor gets small, a → 0. Similar results are found for a massive scalar field in the limit of a large mass" (abstract, verbatim). No reply to VY-II by FLT was found this session (searched; not claimed absent). Position T reads the exchange as: the strong-coupling objection is answered for conformal matter — this program's matter — and open for the graviton (tensor) sector, which is neither conformal nor massive. That open piece is named in §5 as this position's second underspecification; §2 shows the anomaly theory does not close it by itself.

## 2. Does the anomaly-plus-R² theory evade FLT's perturbation problem? No — and the reason is the one that makes the Vilenkin–Yamada answer available

**2.1 The tensor quadratic form on the S⁴ (Rigorous by citation; mapping to J-1's ε Rigorous).** Hawking, Hertog and Reall (PRD 63, 083504 (2001), hep-th/0010232) computed the quadratic action for transverse-traceless perturbations θ_ij of the de Sitter instanton in the theory "Einstein + conformal anomaly of N=4 SYM (via AdS/CFT) + local counterterms," expanded in S⁴ tensor harmonics ∇̂²H^{(p)}_ij = (2 − p(p+3))H^{(p)}_ij, p = 2, 3, …:

S_tensor = (N²/(256π²R⁴)) Σ_p (∫ θ_ij H^{(p)}_ij)² F(p, α, β),  F(p, α, β) = p² + 3p + 6 + Ψ(p) + 2βp(p+1)(p+2)(p+3) − 4αp(p+3),  (HHR 3.81–3.82, verbatim this session)

Ψ(p) = p(p+1)(p+2)(p+3)[ψ(p/2 + 5/2) + ψ(p/2 + 2) − ψ(2) − ψ(1)] + p⁴ + 2p³ − 5p² − 10p − 6  (HHR 3.77, verbatim),

with ψ the digamma function, α the coefficient of their finite R² counterterm S_ct = αN²/(192π²)∫√g R² (HHR 2.9, verbatim), and β the finite part of the Weyl-squared counterterm (HHR 3.15: "S₃ = −(l̄³/64πḠ)(log(l̄/R) − β)∫d⁴x √h (R_ijR^ij − R²/3)"; "the finite part of this term is arbitrary, which is why we have included the constant β", verbatim). Reading of the four pieces: p² + 3p + 6 = (p(p+3) − 2) + 8 is the Einstein operator on TT modes of the unit S⁴; Ψ(p) is the CFT's induced (nonlocal) Weyl term, with Ψ(p) → 2p⁴ ln(p/2) + O(p⁴) at large p (from ψ(x) ~ ln x; Ψ/(2p⁴ ln(p/2)) = 2.5, 1.4, 1.2, 1.1 at p = 10, 40, 100, 1000, script output §6); 2βp(p+1)(p+2)(p+3) is the local Weyl counterterm; −4αp(p+3) is the R² term, which on the Einstein background contributes only 2R̄·δ₂(∫√g R) because δ₁R = 0 for TT modes — a rescaling of the Einstein kinetic term, as the task anticipated.

*Mapping to ε.* HHR's scalar sector factorizes as ψ(2α∇̂² − 1)(∇̂² + 4)ψ (HHR 3.80, verbatim). On a homogeneous mode e^{λt} of de Sitter (H₀ = 1), ∇² → −(λ² + 3λ), so (2α∇² − 1) = 0 gives −2α(λ² + 3λ) = 1 and (∇² + 4) = 0 gives (λ + 4)(λ − 1) = 0. **F(p) = p² + 3p + 6 + Ψ(p) + 2βp(p+1)(p+2)(p+3) + 2εp(p+3).**

The R² coefficient enters with a *positive* sign for the unstable ε > 0: the R² term stiffens the tensor sector; it cannot flip anything.

*Duff's c.* HHR's N=4 SYM has a = c = N²/4 in Duff's normalization ⟨T⟩ = (cC² − aE₄)/(16π²), c = (N_S + 6N_F + 12N_V)/120 (Duff 1994 eq. 31, full text verified in the #216 attack pass; N_S = 6N², N_F = 2N², N_V = N² give c = 30N²/120 ✓). **2.3 FLT's step in this theory (Sketch).** FLT's inverse Gaussian, G_φ[φ₁; 0] ∝ exp[l(l+1)(l+2)φ₁²/(2ℏH²)] × phase (PRL, verbatim), arises because the tensor mode's Euclidean action on the cap, I^{(2)}_E[φ_cl] > 0 for the regular solution, enters the tunneling saddle as e^{+I^{(2)}_E}; on the Hartle–Hawking saddle the same quantity enters as e^{−I^{(2)}_E}. What the fourth-order theory changes is the *operator*, not this sign flip: (i) the local C² term is pointwise non-negative on any real Euclidean region, so its contribution to I^{(2)}_E[φ_cl] on the half-cap is ≥ 0 for β ≥ 0 and, for the admissible β < 0, is dominated by the positive nonlocal piece for every p ≥ 2 (F > 0, §2.2); (ii) the Einstein-plus-R² piece is (1 + 2ε) × FLT's, positive; (iii) with a fourth-order mode equation the regular solutions at the pole form a two-parameter family and the wave function depends on the Ostrogradsky pair (φ₁, φ̇₁); the Gaussian exponent is a 2 × 2 quadratic form whose real part is ±I^{(2)}_E[φ_cl(φ₁, φ̇₁)], negative semi-definite for the HH sign and positive semi-definite for the tunneling sign, because I^{(2)}_E restricted to regular solutions is the minimum of a positive quadratic form. **Verdict: under FLT's regularity condition, the tunneling saddle's tensor exponent is inverted at every l ≥ 2, and it is worse than Einstein gravity by the factor F(l)/(l² + 3l + 6) ≈ 18 (l = 2), 590 (l = 10), 1.2 × 10⁴ (l = 40 ≈ M_P/H₀ at a₂ = 10⁶); the growth is l² ln l.** The theory evades nothing. What *would* evade it — a Weyl coefficient β ≲ −ln(p_*/2) − 1 with p_* ≳ M_P/H₀ (β ≲ −4 at a₂ = 10⁶, ≲ −5 at 10⁷) — is the tachyonic window, excluded by flat-space stability and, in Euclidean terms, the regime where the Hartle–Hawking integral is itself unbounded below. *(The nonlocal piece on a half-manifold is a Sketch-level statement: its sign is read from the closed-sphere F(p); the local pieces are Rigorous.)*

**2.4 The same fact, read the other way (Sketch).** Vilenkin–Yamada's prescription replaces FLT's regularity condition on the modes by "initial quantum state of the field = Euclidean vacuum" (their approach 3), equivalently a Robin condition or a boundary term (their approaches 1–2), and obtains Gaussian-suppressed fluctuations on the tunneling background. So the positivity that makes FLT's inverse Gaussian *steeper* in this theory is precisely the positivity that makes VY's Euclidean vacuum *exist* here; the theory is compatible with the VY resolution for every admissible β, and incompatible with it exactly in the tachyonic window where FLT's problem would have disappeared by itself. A definite statement for the l-range that matters: **for 2 ≤ l ≲ M_P/H₀ the Euclidean tensor form is positive and the Weyl piece dominates it; the tunneling state's tensor sector is Gaussian iff the VY boundary condition is adopted, and inverse-Gaussian iff FLT's is.** What the fourth-order structure adds to VY is a second boundary datum per mode (the Ostrogradsky partner), for which the Euclidean-vacuum condition is the natural extension (the vacuum of a fourth-order Euclidean operator with positive form is unique) but has not been written down; that is the open piece named in §5.

**2.5 The scalar sector, and why T prefers large ε (Sketch; HHR §4.2 verbatim, the α-threshold mapping via α = −ε/2 as in §2.1, the "close to zero" clause reported by the lead from the H-side attack).** HHR: "If α < −1/8 then only the homogeneous (p = 0) negative mode remains," with many scalar negative modes for α negative and close to zero. ## 3. The effective lapse action under the real contour: what was achieved and what was not

**3.1 The J-1 action in the FLT gauge (Rigorous, `lapse_action.py`, `el_onshell.py`).** Write ds² = N_E²dt²/q + q dΩ₃², t ∈ [0, 1], q = a² = e^{2σ}; conformal time is dη = N_E dt/q, so σ′ = qσ̇/N_E and σ″ = (q/N_E)(d/dt)(qσ̇/N_E), and J-1's per-η Lagrangian becomes L_t = (N_E/q)L_η. Lorentzian lapse is N_E = iN with N real: L_t(iN) is i × (real) for real q (checked symbolically), so e^{−I} = e^{iS} with S real on real Lorentzian paths. Results:

- *Truncated sphere.* For the round cap truncated at proper length T (complex T allowed), the action is analytic in T: **I(T)/a = (5/2)(cos T − 1) − ε(cos T − 1)²(cos T + 2)** in J-1's regularization, giving −a(5 + 4ε) at T = π (J-1's full sphere ✓) and −a(5 + 4ε)/2 at T = π/2. The lead's correction (PR #222: J-1's 8(σ′² − 1) subtraction dropped cap-dependent boundary terms; with the unit S⁴ as Riegert reference the round sphere's anomaly piece vanishes) removes the J-1-scheme anomaly piece A(T)/a = −5/6 + (3/4)cos T + (1/12)cos³T and leaves the scheme-independent Einstein and R² pieces: **I(T)/a = −(5/3 + 2ε) + (7/4 + 3ε)cos T − (ε + 1/12)cos³T**, i.e. −a(10/3 + 4ε) at T = π (the corrected sphere action ✓; sympy residual 0). At the FLT-type endpoints T = π/2 ± i arccosh a₁, where a(T) = a₁ and a′(T) = ∓i√(a₁² − 1) (Lorentzian expansion rate), Re I/a = −(5 + 4ε)/2 in J-1's scheme and −(5/3 + 2ε) in the corrected one — in both cases exactly the half-sphere, for both signs — and Im I/a = ∓[(5/2)y + ε(3y + y³)], respectively ∓[(7/4 + 3ε)y + (ε + 1/12)y³], y = √(a₁² − 1): the half-sphere's action plus the Hamilton–Jacobi phase, as in FLT. **I(T) is even in T.** So the tunneling sign cannot be an orientation of the Euclidean segment; on the a′(0) = +1 sheet of the regular series every truncation has Re I = I_half < 0. The sign lives on the other sheet, a′(0) = −1 (a = −τ − c₃τ³ − …, the same geometry with √g → −√g), where I → −I. In FLT's variables this is the sign of Im N_s: their four saddles (3/Λ)(±i ± y) have Re(iS) = ∓12π²/Λ with the sign of Im N_s (`fltcheck.py`, reproducing e^{−12π²/Λ} for Im N_s > 0).
