## 1. Why the Hartle–Hawking sign, and why the Robin definition

### 1.1 The sign is the orientation of Euclidean time at the pole (Rigorous, from DTL)

Di Tucci–Lehners's four saddles for Einstein–Λ are N_{c₁c₂} = (c₁/H²)(√(H²q₁ − 1) + c₂ i) with action S = c₁(4π²/H²)[c₂ i − (H²q₁ − 1)^{3/2}]; the Hartle–Hawking pair (c₁, c₂) = (1, −1), (−1, 1) has Im N < 0 and weight e^{+4π²/H²}. Their Robin condition ℬ = (3π²/N)q̇₀ + α + q₀/β = 0 with q₀ = 0 gives q̇₀ = −αN/(3π²); "From (4) one can see that the initial size q̄₀ vanishes at one (or more) of the saddle points if α = ±6π²i. For α = +6π²i, this occurs at the 'tunnelling' saddle points c₁ = c₂ = 1 and c₁ = c₂ = −1. These are however unstable, so that in the following we will consider α = −6π²i" — which gives q̇₀ = 2iN at "precisely the Hartle-Hawking saddle points" (verbatim, `dtl.txt`). In this program's variables the regular cap has q = a² = τ² + …, ∫a dτ = N_E t, hence q = 2N_E t + O(t²) and q̇₀ = 2N_E. With N = −iN_E (Lorentzian lapse of DTL), q̇₀ = 2iN: **α = −6π²i is pole regularity a′(0) = 1 with Euclidean time oriented so that the Euclidean segment contributes e^{−I}.** The other orientation, α = +6π²i, is the same cap traversed with N → −N, under which S_eff(−N) = −S_eff(N) (the lapse-gauge Lagrangian is odd in N, `lapse_setup.py`), i.e. e^{+I}. The two signs in the fork are the two orientations; nothing else differs at the level of the caps. Verified on the sphere: the continuation τ = π/2 + it to a = 2 gives N_E = 1 + i√3, i.e. DTL's N₃ = √3 − i (`ccaps.py`).

### 1.2 What the fourth-order theory adds: the cone-point divergence (Rigorous algebra, leading order)

For an off-shell geometry with a conical point a ≈ vτ at the pole, the action per a is logarithmically divergent, with coefficient (`position notes, §0.1 formulae`):

  I ⊃ [(v − 1)²/v]·[1 − (3ε + 1)(1 + v)²/4]·ln(1/δ)  →  −3ε (v − 1)² ln(1/δ) as v → 1,

the Γ term contributing +(v − 1)²/v (from (□σ)² ~ 4(v − 1)²/θ⁴ on the reference sphere) and the R² term −(3ε + 1)(1 − v²)²/(4v) (from R ~ 6(1 − v²)/(v²τ²) with κ < 0). For the physical sign ε > 0 the coefficient is negative near v = 1: cone points are infinitely enhanced under e^{−I} and infinitely suppressed under e^{+I}. This is Hawking–Hertog–Reall's "the presence of the R² counterterm with a negative coefficient appears to make matters even worse" (verbatim, `hhr.txt`), made quantitative. Consequences for the two definitions:

- A Dirichlet, Feldbrugge–Lehners–Turok-type integral over all q(t) with q₀ = 0 sums over cone points at the pole; in this theory that sum does not exist for either sign as an integral over real geometries — under e^{−I} it diverges on cones, under e^{+I} it converges on cones but carries FLT's unsuppressed on-shell perturbations, which no contour choice removes ("the problem of unbounded perturbations, at small wavelengths, is unavoidable", FLT, verified earlier this cycle).
- Di Tucci–Lehners define the integral on thimbles and explicitly require the thimble to avoid singular geometries: "we would like to avoid summing over such geometries, as they are (infinitely) sensitive to the addition of higher curvature terms. This will imply an upper bound on |β|" (verbatim). In the fourth-order theory this exclusion is not a refinement but the condition of existence, and once it is imposed the pole of every geometry on the thimble is regular *exactly*: regularity is not a saddle-point outcome (as in FLT, where the N-saddle enforces q̇₀ = 2iN) but a property of the contour. That is the strongest form of the Robin / Euclidean-momentum definition, and it is what Position H adopts.

*(The extension of DTL's thimble construction to the fourth-order lapse integral is not carried out here; §3.5 says exactly what stands in the way. Its existence is a Conjecture of this position.)*

### 1.3 The premise argument (Judgment)

The reframing's root is the state of minimum excitation. Under the tunneling sign the tensor modes are "an inverse Gaussian distribution" (FLT, verified earlier), so the state is not one of minimum excitation, whatever the caps do. Under the Hartle–Hawking sign via Robin, DTL verify "Gaussian distributed perturbations with a scale-invariant spectrum … the model is stable against small deviations since large values of φ₁ are suppressed" (verbatim). A program whose root is defined as the low-excitation end has already chosen; Position H makes the choice explicit and pays its price (item 4 of the summary) rather than adopting the tunneling sign to rescue the sphere's dominance.

## 2. Which saddles contribute, as a function of the final 3-geometry

### 2.1 The wavefunction has two arguments (Rigorous structure)

Regularity at the pole fixes three of the four Cauchy data of the fourth-order equation (q₀ = 0, q̇₀ = 2N_E, q⃛₀ tied to q̈₀), leaving c₃ (`lapse_check.py`: the t-series q = 2Nt + 6c₃N²t² + 2N³c₃(6c₃ + 1)t³/(3ε) + … reproduces the τ-series exactly). A final 3-geometry with the scalaron datum, (q₁, R₁) or equivalently (q₁, q̇₁), then fixes both c₃ and the cut point τ₁ — complex in general. So Ψ(q₁, R₁) is a sum over isolated saddles labelled by branch, and the lapse is an output (N_E = ∫₀^{τ₁} a dτ), not an integration variable that survives semiclassically. This is the fourth-order theory's version of FLT's structure; Hawking–Luttrell 1984 treat the R² minisuperspace with the R² term as a massive scalar (existence verified; content at abstract level only, unverified at full text), and Vilenkin 1985 solves the Wheeler–DeWitt equation of the Starobinsky model for a tunneling wavefunction (existence and abstract verified) — both consistent with a two-argument Ψ, neither addressing the double bubble.

### 2.3 Amplitude versus measure, and conditioning (committed)

### 3.2 The structure of S_eff(N) at fixed q₁ (Rigorous)

With the pole regular and only q₁ fixed, the caps ending at q₁ form a one-complex-parameter family, c₃ ↦ (τ₁(c₃), N_E(c₃)); S_eff(N) := I(c₃(N)). Its stationary points in N are the stationary points of I along the family, which by the Hamilton–Jacobi relation dI = −ℋ dτ₁ + p_a da₁ + p_{a′} da′₁ are where the momentum conjugate to the *unfixed* final datum vanishes (p_{a′}-type for the Ostrogradsky choice, K = 0 for the (q, R) choice with its Legendre boundary term). These are the marginal-over-R₁ saddles, a derived object; the physical saddles of Ψ(q₁, R₁) are every point of the family. There is no FLT-type isolated lapse saddle at fixed q₁ in the fourth-order theory: the lapse integral's job (enforcing regularity) has already been done by the contour.

### 3.4 The frame ambiguity of partial anomaly actions (a finding; Rigorous (numerical))

The map θ(τ) to the reference sphere solves a first-order equation singular at the pole, dθ/dτ ≈ θ/τ, so θ ≈ Cτ with C free: the SO(1,1) boost of S⁴'s conformal group. Γ of a *closed* cap is C-independent (§0.2). Γ of a cap *cut* at τ₁ is not: for the ε = 1 double bubble, Γ(pole → neck) = −0.17, −0.91, −1.16, −1.23 at C = ½, 1, 2, 4, and the pole-normalized frame C = 1 puts the neck at θ = 2.56 rather than π/2. In the frame with θ(τ_n) = π/2 (C = 0.298), I(pole → neck) = −4.32377 = I_DB/2 exactly, and Re I stays at that value along the whole real recollapsing continuation (`frame_check.out`); the same frame for the sphere is C = 1. So: **the weight of a classical history rooted at a reflection-symmetric K = 0 junction is unambiguous, e^{−I_full}, and the real part of the action is frozen along the history; the amplitude at a generic 3-geometry is not determined by the geometry's action alone.** Physically, the anomaly-induced action on a manifold with boundary carries boundary contributions that depend on the conformal fields' boundary state, which the minisuperspace-of-geometry reduction cannot supply. This is the precise form of the boundary-term issue the lead anticipated, and it bounds what any lapse map in this theory can mean off the junctions. (Every partial action in §3.3 and §2.2 is quoted in the pole-normalized frame, with its local EH + R² part — frame-free — separated in the raw output.)

### 3.5 Not achieved, and why

1. **Thimbles.** A Picard–Lefschetz decomposition needs S_eff(N) with isolated saddles and a defining contour; §3.2 shows the first is absent at fixed q₁ and §3.4 shows the exponent is frame-dependent off the junctions. The route that remains is the (q₁, R₁)-wavefunction with a regularized Robin term at the pole (below) and the matter boundary state at the cut — two ingredients, both named, neither supplied.
2. **The Robin term itself.** DTL's ℬ contains p_q(0) with finite α; here p_q ~ 1/(2π²q) at every regular pole (§3.1). A Robin condition must be written for q·p_q or in the scalaron representation; c₃ is invisible to it in either form (§4). The analog of β_max — the thimble avoiding the cone locus — is now mandatory rather than optional (§1.2).
3. **A plotted thimble picture in the N-plane.** Both maps exist as tables (`qfam_*.pkl`); without isolated saddles (§3.2) there is nothing for steepest-descent lines to connect, so no plot was made. The N-plane region covered at q₁ = 4 contains DTL's N₃ (the sphere) and its complex neighbours only.

## 4. Both caps satisfy the Robin condition (Rigorous algebra)

The J-6 draft's transcription (its §1.2) states q ≈ Nt and dq/dt → N; the correct leading behaviour is q = 2N_E t + 6c₃N_E²t² + …, so q̇₀ = 2N_E = 2iN in DTL's lapse — which is *exactly* DTL's condition ℬ = 0 at q₀ = 0 with α = −6π²i. The factor does not change the conclusion; it makes it exact. All three pole data that a Robin condition of DTL's type can touch — q₀ = 0, q̇₀ = 2N_E, p_{q̇}(0) = 0 (from §3.1's formula with q = 0, q̇ = 2N) — are identical for every regular cap; c₃ first appears in q̈₀ = 12c₃N_E², the initial *velocity* of the second Ostrogradsky variable, which no boundary condition at the pole fixes and which the final data determine. The remaining pole datum, p_q(0), diverges identically for all regular caps (§3.1) and cannot select among them either. Hence the Robin / Euclidean-momentum definition admits both caps as saddles, each at its own final data, both with the Hartle–Hawking sign, and J-1's ordering (corrected §0) is what it compares. What the definition *does* select is the orientation (α's sign) and, through β, which of the conjugate saddles is on the thimble; it cannot see the neck.

## 5. Stability of the double-bubble saddle (Sketch)

DTL's "stable" has two components: the saddle is *relevant* (on the thimble the defining contour deforms to) and its perturbations are Gaussian-suppressed. What can be said here:

1. **Minisuperspace, c₃ direction.** The only regular closed caps are the sphere and the symmetric double bubble (HHR: "if α < 0 then there are two regular compact instantons, namely the round four sphere and a new 'double bubble' instanton", verbatim; J-1 confirms). Their neighbours in c₃ close at a conical south pole (HHR's "irregular South pole"), where §1.2's coefficient −3ε(v − 1)² ln(1/δ) applies with v = |a′| at the south pole: the closed-cap action falls to −∞ on both sides of *each* regular cap. So along c₃ neither is a local minimum, and the direction is excluded by the thimble prescription for both alike. This is the negative-coefficient R² unboundedness, not a discriminator; it is also why a "negative mode" computation in the usual Coleman–de Luccia sense is ill-posed here (the would-be bounce has *lower* action than the background, B = ΔI < 0).
2. **At fixed final data.** Each saddle is isolated in (c₃, τ₁); relevance is then a question about the lapse-direction Hessian and the contour, which §3.5 leaves open.
3. **As a classical history.** The neck continuation is real with Re I frozen (§2.2, §3.4): in Hartle–Hawking–Hertog's sense it is a classical history of the state, exactly as the sphere's is. The open continuation is a runaway. Neither is unstable in the sense of a growing complex direction along the history; both simply end.
4. **Perturbations.** "We shall not have much to say about the new instanton in this paper since the lack of an analytical solution makes dealing with perturbations of this instanton rather difficult" (HHR, verbatim); "An O(4) approach would also be necessary to investigate the double bubble instanton" (verbatim). Not done here either. HHR also write "This could give support to the idea that the double bubble instanton describes an instability of the de Sitter vacuum" (verbatim) — under Position H, with ΔI < 0, that reading is not a tunneling rate but the statement that the state prefers the neck; a perturbation analysis showing a non-Gaussian mode on the double bubble would be the one result that could remove it from the Robin-defined sum without touching the sphere, and is the computation this position most needs.

## 7. Falsifiers, axiom check, underspecification

**Falsifiers of Position H (each a computation, not a taste):**
1. A perturbation analysis on the double-bubble background showing that under the Robin definition its tensor modes are *not* Gaussian-suppressed while the sphere's are — the double bubble would then be an irrelevant saddle and the ε bound would dissolve without changing the sign.
2. A demonstration that the thimble of the fourth-order lapse integral through the sphere's saddle at some fixed (q₁, R₁) does *not* pass through the double bubble's at the neck data with the same sign — i.e. that relevance is decided differently for the two families.
3. A scan in ε showing the double bubble's open continuation becomes a graceful-exit inflationary universe for some ε > ε_c (checked only at ε = 1 here); Position H's "no root above ε_c" would then need the closed/open junction question of J-3 answered first.
4. A one-loop prefactor ratio between the two caps exceeding e^{0.12a} near ε_c — the corrected margin is small enough at birth that this is no longer absurd.
5. The corrected Γ failing a further independent check (e.g. against a heat-kernel evaluation of the conformal-field determinant on the double bubble); §0.2's tests make this unlikely but it is the load-bearing computation of this note.

**Axiom / hidden-assumption check.** No evolution is asserted: the histories are classical branches of saddles, Re I frozen along them (verified), and "time" on a branch is the WKB parameter of J-5. The parameter t of the lapse gauge is pure gauge (N_E = ∫a dτ is an output of the cap, §3.2). The FRW minisuperspace foliation is inherited and disclosed (J-1 caveat ii); the frame ambiguity of §3.4 shows the foliation's cut is *not* innocent for the matter sector — a preferred-surface dependence that the program must own at J-4. No background is smuggled: the reference S⁴ of §0 is a computational device whose choice drops out of every closed-cap quantity (verified) and is flagged wherever it does not. No preferred observer enters; conditioning (§2.3) is named as the one place one could.

**Underspecified (named):** (i) the thimble construction for the fourth-order lapse integral (§3.5.1); (ii) the regularized Robin term (§3.5.2); (iii) the conformal matter's boundary state at a generic cut, hence Ψ(q₁, R₁) off the junctions (§3.4); (iv) perturbations around the double bubble (§5.4); (v) the ε-dependence of the open continuation (§2.2, checked at ε = 1 only); (vi) one-loop prefactors, now relevant near ε_c (§2.3); (vii) J-1's N_inf seed estimate, inherited.
