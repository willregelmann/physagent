## Flaw 1 — cones: tunneling sign not protected for ε < 1

**Verdict: narrower-viable.** The note's sentence is false as written (the attack's sign map and quadrature are right); a precise asymmetry survives.

**Evidence** (`stm_cone.out`): the bracket 3ε(v+1)² + (v−1)(v+3) has the single positive root **v*(ε) = 2/√(1+3ε) − 1** (sympy; bracket vanishes there identically), so c(v) − c(1) > 0 (I → +∞) for 0 < v < v*(ε) and < 0 (I → −∞) for v > v*(ε), v ≠ 1. v*(ε) > 0 iff ε < 1; v*(1) = 0, and at ε = 1 the coefficient is −(v−1)²(v+2) ≤ 0 for every v > 0. Values: v* = 0.512, 0.451, 0.392, 0.265, 0.085, 0 at ε = 0.25, 0.3, 0.355, 0.5, 0.8, 1 (matches `atk_cone.out`). Sector decomposition: R² sector −3ε(v²−1)²/(4v) ≤ 0 for all v (both signs of v − 1); anomaly sector −(v−1)³(v+3)/(4v), > 0 for v < 1, < 0 for v > 1 (the Casimir-like cost of a narrow cone). Expansions: near v = 1, −3ε(v−1)² − (v−1)³ + O((v−1)⁴); v → 0⁺, (3/4)(1−ε)/v; v → ∞, −(3ε+1)v³/4.

**Surviving statement (paste):** "For ε > 0 the smoothed-cone exponent is unbounded under e^{−I} in every neighbourhood of the regular pole (coefficient −3ε(v−1)² on both sides of v = 1) and on every wide cone; under e^{+I} it is bounded on v*(ε) < v < ∞, a full neighbourhood of the regular pole, and unbounded only on needle cones 0 < v < v*(ε) = 2/√(1+3ε) − 1 (0.51–0.39 across H's window), a set bounded away from v = 1 for every ε > 0 and empty for ε ≥ 1. At the benchmark ε = 1 the tunneling sign is protected against every cone; inside H's window it is not. The R² sector is what enhances cones under e^{−I}; the anomaly sector is what enhances needles under e^{+I}. This is an asymmetry of *where* in cone-angle each sign fails, not a one-sided protection." Rigor row: "Rigorous (sympy + numerical), minisuperspace; the sign consequence as restated".
